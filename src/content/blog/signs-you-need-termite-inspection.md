Termites cause billions of dollars in damage each year, and South Texas has active subterranean termite populations. Catching activity early can save major repair costs.

## 1. Mud tubes

Pencil-width tubes of soil on foundation walls or piers are classic signs of subterranean termites traveling between soil and wood.

## 2. Hollow-sounding wood

Tap baseboards, window frames or sill plates. Damaged wood often sounds hollow or papery.

## 3. Discarded wings

After a swarm, piles of discarded wings near windows or doors can indicate a nearby colony.

## 4. Frass or pellets

Some termites leave dry fecal pellets that look like tiny wood-colored grains.

## 5. Visible damage or soft spots

Bubbling paint, warped wood or areas that give under pressure deserve a professional look.
